## Look for problems such as

- confusing, vague, bloated, repetitive, or misleading wording;
- concepts used before they are explained, circular explanations, or missing bridges;
- a note that belongs earlier, later, or beside a different topic;
- unclear, inconsistent, or misleading topic names;
- weak navigation, bad links, awkward grouping, or an ugly/unclear order;
- examples that do not expose the idea, missing counterexamples, or practice that tests recall instead of understanding;
- unexplained notation, assumptions, edge cases, units, dimensions, or limits;
- incorrect mathematics, science, code, or reasoning;
- poor Markdown, MathJax, diagrams, spacing, accessibility, searchability, or generated-page display;

## Improve the notes

Make explanations direct and precise. Define ideas before relying on them. Separate intuition from definitions, rules, proofs, derivations, approximations, and examples when that distinction prevents confusion. Explain why a method works, when it applies, and how to recognize when it does not. Use the simplest effective example, and add a contrasting case or common mistake when it prevents a likely misunderstanding.

Check that practice moves from recognizing the idea to using it, explaining it, diagnosing mistakes, and transferring it to an unfamiliar situation. Add or revise practice only when it materially improves understanding, following the repositoryâ€™s existing convention for answers or solution checkpoints.

For mathematical or scientific notes, check assumptions, domains, notation, signs, units, dimensions, limiting cases, and claims. For technical notes, check current behavior against repository evidence or authoritative documentation when needed. Do not preserve a technically correct statement if its presentation predictably causes misunderstanding; rewrite it or add the missing condition.

If a structural change is justified, update the relevant source files, names, links, manifests, prerequisite maps, and navigation together. Keep identifiers stable unless changing one clearly improves usability and all references can be updated safely.

## Final report

Keep the report concise:

- **Fixed:** numbered problems, with the learning benefit and files changed.
- **Validation:** commands run and their actual results; mention generated files rebuilt.
- **Not fixed:** important discovered problems, why they were deferred, and their priority.
- **Next run:** the 3 most valuable remaining improvements to investigate.

End with a clear statement of any unresolved risk or uncertainty. The goal is continuous improvement of the fastest path to understanding, not completion of a textbook checklist.
