# Manufacturing

Manufacturing turns material into useful products by choosing processes that fit the design, the volume, and the cost target.

## Core ideas

- Process selection depends on geometry, material, accuracy, and production scale.
- Tolerances and surface finish affect both function and cost.
- Manufacturing quality depends on repeatability, tooling, and inspection.

## Common methods and terms

- Common process families include machining, forming, casting, welding, and additive manufacturing.
- Automation improves throughput when the process is stable and repeatable.
- Design for manufacturability tries to reduce difficult features before production starts.

## Typical questions

- Which process best matches the part geometry?
- What cost is driven by tolerance or finish requirements?
- Can the part be produced reliably at the needed volume?
