1.3Review: Rigid body idealization

Rigid body idealization

What is the main idea of Rigid body idealization?

-

Solution

One short answer is: A rigid body does not deform under load. Distances between points remain fixed. This assumption is usually valid when deformation is small enough that it does not affect force balance.

1.4Review: Particle idealization

Particle idealization

What is the main idea of Particle idealization?

-

Solution

One short answer is: A particle has mass but no size. It can translate, but it cannot rotate. Use particle equilibrium when only the net force matters and moments are irrelevant.

1.7Review: Force vectors

Force vectors

What is the main idea of Force vectors?

-

Solution

One short answer is: A force has magnitude, direction, and point of application. In Cartesian form: $$ \mathbf{F} = F x \mathbf{i} + F y \mathbf{j} + F z \mathbf{k} $$ The magnitude is $$ \mathbf{F} = \sqrt{F x^2 + F y^2 + F z^2} $$

1.8Review: Moment of a force

Moment of a force

What is the main idea of Moment of a force?

-

Solution

One short answer is: The moment of a force about point $O$ is $$ \mathbf{M} O = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F} $$ where $\mathbf{r}$ is the position vector from $O$ to the point of application.

1.9Review: Couple moment

Couple moment

What is the main idea of Couple moment?

-

Solution

One short answer is: A couple consists of two equal and opposite parallel forces separated by a distance.

1.10Review: Varignon's theorem

Varignon's theorem

What is the main idea of Varignon's theorem?

-

Solution

One short answer is: The moment of a resultant force about a point equals the sum of the moments of its components about that point. This is useful when breaking a force into components before taking moments. ---

1.11Review: Equilibrium equations

4. Equilibrium equations

What is the main idea of Equilibrium equations?

-

Solution

One short answer is: Equilibrium requires both translational and rotational balance.

1.12Review: Particle equilibrium

Particle equilibrium

What is the main idea of Particle equilibrium?

-

Solution

One short answer is: For a particle in 2D: $$ \sum F x = 0, \qquad \sum F y = 0 $$ For a particle in 3D: $$ \sum F x = 0, \qquad \sum F y = 0, \qquad \sum F z = 0 $$

1.13Review: Rigid-body equilibrium in 2D

Rigid-body equilibrium in 2D

What is the main idea of Rigid-body equilibrium in 2D?

-

Solution

One short answer is: The three independent equations are: $$ \sum F x = 0 $$ $$ \sum F y = 0 $$ $$ \sum M O = 0 $$ The moment may be taken about any convenient point $O$.

1.14Review: Rigid-body equilibrium in 3D

Rigid-body equilibrium in 3D

What is the main idea of Rigid-body equilibrium in 3D?

-

Solution

One short answer is: The six equations are: $$ \sum F x = 0,\ \sum F y = 0,\ \sum F z = 0 $$ $$ \sum M x = 0,\ \sum M y = 0,\ \sum M z = 0 $$ These are the only independent equilibrium equations for a rigid body. ---

-

Solution

-

Solution

3.8Review: Direction of friction

Direction of friction

What is the main idea of Direction of friction?

-

Solution

One short answer is: Friction acts opposite the direction of impending or actual relative motion. Do not assume friction always points opposite the applied force. It opposes the motion the contact surface would otherwise have.
