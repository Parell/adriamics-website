ROCINANTE

A reusable electric tug concept for moving, servicing, and handling spacecraft in orbit.

The idea

Orbital logistics infrastructure

Move payloads
Keep the tug

Rocinante is an early concept for a reusable spacecraft that can rendezvous with payloads, capture or support them, and transport them between orbits using electric propulsion.

The architecture combines high-power solar-electric propulsion, modular cargo interfaces, robotic handling, and long-duration propellant storage. Every figure below is a preliminary design target, not a validated specification.

Design priorities

Navigate and communicate

Study omnidirectional S-band communications and navigation for rendezvous and proximity operations.

Capture and handle

Explore free-flying robotic arms, an adjustable pusher plate, and optional elastic tethers for payload capture and stacking.

Generate and distribute power

Compare deployable solar arrays and lightweight concentrators, high-voltage DC distribution, and power electronics near the thrusters.

Manage propellant and heat

Investigate CO₂ storage as a solid or liquid, controlled delivery to thrusters, tank integration, and active thermal control.

Study targets

For architecture trade studies only

Vehicle sizing

Initial allocations: at about 2,000 kg or less for good perfromance.

Power and propulsion

Explore 200-400 kW solar generation, a small eclipse battery, and CO₂ electric propulsion with a notional specific impulse in the 2,000-second range. Efficiency and lifetime remain open questions.

Propellant storage

Early scenarios consider 4,000 kg of CO₂ and an optional 9,000 kg extended-range tank, with storage concepts spanning solid CO₂ and cryogenic liquids.

Integrated structure

Study tank-as-truss layouts with thermal, electrical, and structural functions integrated into common assemblies, plus separation and insulation approaches.

These values have not been reconciled into a complete mass, power, thermal, or mission budget. They should not be read as offered performance.
